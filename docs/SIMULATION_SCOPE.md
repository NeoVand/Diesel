# Engineering model, teaching mechanism and evidence

> Scope note: this document describes the historical Caterpillar prototype. The purchased generic V12 has a separate [native audit](../references/00_Active_V12/V12_NATIVE_AUDIT.md) and [migration plan](../references/00_Active_V12/APP_MIGRATION_AUDIT.md); these old specifications do not apply to it.

The engineering reference is the **51.8 L, 170 × 190 mm, 1800 rpm / 60 Hz Caterpillar 3512C electric-power configuration**. The application combines purchased generic 3512 exterior geometry, authored educational internals, and a bounded published performance map. No actual engine serial, complete arrangement or installed ECM software has been identified. This is a working teaching demonstration with source-grounded steady operating data; it is not a validated production digital twin or transient engine simulator.

## Fixed-speed performance reference

`src/lib/engine/simulation.ts` contains twelve **EM1898-00** anchors transcribed from `references/99_Archive/Caterpillar_3512_2026-09-29/03_Performance_Emissions_TMI/EM1898-00_1800rpm_steady_state_map.csv`. The controlling source is [PERF005: Caterpillar PDS](https://emc.cat.com/pubdirect.ashx?media_string_id=PDS-EM1898-00-GS-EPG-10343290.pdf), pages 1–2, with matched conditions in [PERF009: Caterpillar TSS](https://emc.cat.com/pubdirect.ashx?media_string_id=TSS-EM1898-00-GS-EPG-10343422.pdf).

The selected baseline is **1500 ekW standby / 1875 kVA / 0.8 PF**, feature 512DRC5, provisional generator arrangement 3838418, 480 V, IE/frame 1447. It is nominal source evidence, not an identified machine measurement. Standby does not establish continuous-duty permission. Prime, continuous and newer performance revisions remain separate.

The load control covers **10–100% electrical load at fixed 1800 rpm**. Exact anchors are reported; intermediate points are interpolated along adjacent straight segments. Nonfinite, idle, off-speed and overload inputs are rejected. A changing display or load control does not predict governor response, turbo lag, frequency dip, warm-up or a real load step.

| Electrical load | Electrical output | Engine brake power | Fuel flow | BSFC         |
| --------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ | --------- | ------------ |
| 25%             | 375 ekW           | 471.4 bkW          | 126.7 L/h | 228.4 g/bkWh |
| 50%             | 750 ekW           | 853.0 bkW          | 216.9 L/h | 216.1 g/bkWh |
| 75%             | 1125 ekW          | 1239.1 bkW         | 306.4 L/h | 210.2 g/bkWh |
| 100%            | 1500 ekW          | 1644.9 bkW         | 390.8 L/h | 201.9 g/bkWh |

Temperature locations remain distinct: `exhaustC` is stack temperature (402.6 °C at full load), while `exhaustManifoldC` is manifold temperature (618.6 °C). Volumetric flows retain their printed units and unresolved reference conditions.

Average shaft torque is `brake kW × 1000 / (rpm × 2π / 60)`. LHV fuel input is `L/h × 0.85 kg/L × 42,780 kJ/kg / 3600`, using PERF009's **850 g/L at 15 °C** and **42,780 kJ/kg lower heating value**; full-load input is about **3947.4 kW**. `efficiencyPercent` is electrical output divided by LHV input, about **38.0%** at full load; `brakeEfficiencyPercent` instead uses reported brake power. Neither is alternator efficiency. Brake/electrical differences include unresolved package loads and boundaries. BSFC and volumetric fuel remain independently reported/interpolated channels.

## Mechanical teaching clock

`src/lib/engine/mechanism.ts` evaluates a true slider-crank, with 95 mm crank radius and assumed 350 mm pin-to-pin rod length. The 190 mm stroke and constant rod length are deterministic constraints. Ideal bore/stroke displacement is approximately **51.751 L total / 4.313 L per cylinder**; the nominal 14.7:1 ratio gives approximately **0.315 L ideal clearance volume**. These calculated volumes do not reconstruct the rendered piston bowl or production chamber.

| Local cylinder phase | Instructional event                                 |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–180°               | Intake / descending piston                          |
| 180–360°             | Compression / rising piston                         |
| 350–370°             | Assumed injection indication around compression TDC |
| 360–540°             | Power / descending piston                           |
| 540–720°             | Exhaust / rising piston                             |

Piston geometry repeats after 360°; the complete valve/injection cycle repeats after 720°. The teaching valves have zero lift at event boundaries and no overlap. Real opening/closing angles, lift profiles and injection rate are unknown. Chamber glow is an authored indication, not cylinder pressure or a heat-release curve.

The teaching chamber now uses a bounded translucent charge volume and a phase-gated spray, both contained above the instantaneous piston crown and below the head/nozzle. The injection cue is visible at a paused instructional injection phase when Fuel is enabled; the early power-stroke indication also remains inspectable while paused. Charge color changes from cool blue toward warm amber with geometric compression and toward orange during the authored combustion window. This is an explanation of compression ignition—not a spark, flame-temperature measurement, spray breakup calculation or ignition-delay calibration. Separated/exploded chambers suppress these cues rather than showing combustion in disconnected parts. All cues obey the active section plane. Paused 355° highlights assumed injection; 420° highlights the power-stroke charge indication. Exact injector focus in the assembled Fuel cylinder study includes the nozzle, chamber, head and instantaneous piston crown in its camera bounds; it does not enlarge the spray or change geometric clearances.

The physical teaching principle is supported by the [U.S. Department of Energy's internal-combustion overview](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/vehicles/articles/internal-combustion-engine-basics) and [Cummins' diesel-engine explanation](https://www.cummins.com/en-na/news/how-do-diesel-engines-work): the piston compresses air, metered fuel is sprayed into that hot charge, and compression ignition produces the power stroke without a spark plug. These references establish the principle, not the 3512C's production timing or geometry.

At the reference speed there are 30 crank revolutions/s, 15 cycles/s per cylinder and 180 events/s for twelve firing cylinders; mean piston speed is 11.4 m/s. **Playback speed changes the animation, not reference rpm or the performance map.** Phase uses elapsed time independently of capped visual-transition damping. Pausing freezes the cycle and tracers; seeking re-evaluates the mechanism at the requested angle.

The representative cylinder offset is `(cylinder number − 1) × 60°`. Local phase is `wrap(global phase − offset, 720)`. For example, cylinder 02 at global 210° is at local 150° / intake; global 360° places it at local 300° / compression. Focused `cylinder` labels and stepping use the visible cylinder's local phase; whole-engine section and mechanism transport use cylinder 01 as the global reference. MCP `seek` uses the global phase. A numbered selection can expose its own separately qualified local phase to the guide. These offsets are explicitly educational and do not establish Caterpillar firing order.

## Shared state, teaching and the guide

UI controls, authored lessons and acknowledged agent commands use the same `LabState` / `LabAction` reducer. Discrete intents advance revision; render frames do not. State snapshots sample actual phase and camera separately from the last requested seek. Stable source `Frame_*` IDs and educational `mech:*` IDs support selection, focus, isolation, hiding and illustrative removal.

Scene checkpoints preserve display, section axis/position/direction/guide, internal visibility, component visibility, explosion, reference load, run/playback, phase and camera. They do not resume an old agent turn, restore synthesized-audio playback position or reproduce a lesson timer. Manual interruption and Stop cancel active guidance rather than allowing queued commands to continue.

Authored lessons in `src/lib/engine/lessons.ts` coordinate sections, cycle events, flow overlays and short explanations. The guide queries registered identities and current sampled state, executes one bounded command at a time, and waits for browser acknowledgement. Its final explanation must use the observed display and focused-cylinder phase. The curated knowledge pack contains independently written facts/paraphrases and source links; retained manuals are not ingested or published wholesale. Generated speech is narration, not engine/component acoustics.

## Evidence boundaries and review status

- **Pressure / flow basis:** manifold pressure's gauge-versus-absolute convention and some volumetric-flow conditions are unresolved. Do not infer mass flow or calibrate boost dynamics from raw columns.
- **Heat balance:** combining brake, jacket, atmosphere, exhaust and TSS aftercooler channels gives approximately 4187.6 kW, about 6% above matched fuel input. Channel boundaries and oil-cooler overlap remain unresolved. Do not force conservation or count oil-cooler heat twice.
- **Physical properties:** alloys, heat treatment, conductivity, heat capacity, emissivity, strength and fatigue data are unavailable. Native `Generic` materials and placeholder densities are not physical evidence.
- **Geometry:** native product-label matching improves provenance, not serial applicability. Source visual-export coordinates are scaled relative to STEP millimeters; authored teaching geometry has its own display scale. Their visual relationship is not a physically registered overlay. See [asset pipeline](ASSET_PIPELINE.md) and [native recovery audit](../references/99_Archive/Caterpillar_3512_2026-09-29/09_Geometry_Integration/NATIVE_CAD_RECOVERY.md).
- **Dynamics / controls:** no calibrated turbo maps, inertias, governor gains, combustion delay, thermal capacitances, alternator/excitation model or protection configuration exists. No validated start, idle, load-step, warm-up, emissions-map or fault-prediction capability is claimed.

The independent review corrected closed-valve/injection tracer leakage, pin/bearing interference, rod axial overlap, the fabricated continuous shaft crossing rods at BDC, frame-rate-dependent cycle advance, repeated camera resets caused by equivalent snapshot objects, focused-cylinder/global-phase mismatch, and stale phase labels on unrelated agent actions. Nominal slider-crank endpoint constraints and TDC head/valve clearance are coherent for the authored geometry; this is not manufacturing-clearance validation. Camera restoration now clears previous orbit/pan/dolly inertia before applying the saved pose; a seeded OrbitControls check remained at the requested pose over 120 subsequent updates (maximum numerical error about 3.202e-15 display units). A 721-phase sweep across all twelve teaching cylinders verified positive head/valve clearance; those checks validate authored geometry rather than production tolerances.

Development evidence: [geometry verification report](verification/geometry-verification.json) records the 721 × 12 sweep, 24 camera fits and closed-event flow gates; [camera restoration check](verification/camera-verification.json) seeds pending orbit/pan/zoom inertia and verifies the saved pose over 120 updates. The minimum authored valve/head clearances were 7.50 / 17.33 mm at the teaching scale. These local development artifacts validate the implementation and do not establish Caterpillar manufacturing dimensions.

A subsequent CPU mesh audit corrected adjacent-liner wall overlap, rod-bore bevel encroachment on its bearing sleeves and an oversized spray penetrating the piston near TDC. Authored head/deck and cam skins are suppressed in intact source assembly/section views unless explicitly inspected; they return when separated or in a standalone teaching study. The finite liners and piston skirts remain radially outside the authored crank throw envelope across 721 sampled angles for all twelve cylinders. The source casting, manifold and cover placement are not physically registered to that rig, so this proof does not establish collision clearance against every commercial source body. Twenty focused mechanism/scene cases now cover local envelopes, phase gating, paused cues, clipping, cover suppression, closed solids, retained bores and contextual injector framing.

The stricter [authored mechanical audit](../references/99_Archive/Caterpillar_3512_2026-09-29/09_Geometry_Integration/AUTHORED_MECHANICAL_AUDIT.json) repaired an open piston underside rim, open swept-wire ends, reversed liner cut-side winding, a rod contour with insufficient wall around its bearing aperture, detached bolt positions and a wrist pin extending into an unbored skirt. The piston retains a real underside cavity and annular pin bosses. The injector has a real enlarged deck aperture with positive surrounding wall material. All **718 constituent solids** passed the oriented-edge/positive-volume closure audit; the final aperture repair passed the same regression again. The six older manually positioned cylinder-study bed/crank cap meshes were retired after a rendered contributor A/B confirmed duplicate face interference; both clipping planes now use the actual solid-winding caps, while all physical bed/crank meshes remain unchanged.

A later [piston shading verification](verification/2026-09-29-v5-piston-shading.json) corrected smoothed normals on actual flat lathe faces and coincident poles; the piston aluminium finish alone is now isotropic because its angular cap UV derivatives produced radial stripes. All 264 mesh position/UV/index buffers, triangle order and constituent ranges remain unchanged across 795,996 position vertices. Only the twelve piston normal buffers and their display finish changed. The historical mechanical audit and its measured geometry remain unchanged; this shading repair adds no production-fidelity claim.

The actual triangle-boundary sweep evaluated **721 poses / 242,256 mesh-pair checks**, with no nonzero rod/crank, paired-rod or rod/piston boundary intersection. It did not audit all source bodies or all head/cam contacts. Actual axial intervals additionally keep rods clear of non-joint crank solids throughout shaft rotation. Shared crank-pin centers and wrist-pin centers are intentional bearing joints; their real annular openings retain positive radial clearance.
